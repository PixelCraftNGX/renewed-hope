Reported progress

10 Things President Tinubu Has Done for Nigeria

These are major areas of action under the Tinubu administration. Where work is ongoing or planned, it is clearly labelled as such.

1Completed

Economic Reform and Fiscal Stability

The administration removed the unsustainable fuel subsidy and unified the foreign-exchange market. These reforms were designed to reduce wasteful subsidies, improve public revenue, stabilise the economy, and redirect resources toward infrastructure and social investment.

What it means for Nigerians: More public resources can be directed to roads, schools, healthcare, agriculture, and support for vulnerable households. The reforms also brought short-term cost-of-living pressure, which the government says it is addressing through targeted support, CNG transport, agriculture, and job-creation programmes. [1][5]

Official source
2Expanding

Student Financial Support Through NELFUND

The Nigerian Education Loan Fund was created to help eligible Nigerian students access higher education without being excluded because of financial hardship. The administration reports support for more than 1.5 million students and disbursements of more than ₦282 billion. [1]

What it means for Nigerians: Students and families receive support for tuition and upkeep, reducing financial barriers to tertiary education and expanding opportunity for young Nigerians.

Official source
3Ongoing

Major Road and Transport Infrastructure

The administration is investing in highways, bridges, rural access roads, and transport corridors. Priority projects include the Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway, Sokoto–Badagry Superhighway, Abuja–Kaduna–Zaria–Kano Road, East–West Road, and rural road programmes.

What it means for Nigerians: Better roads can shorten journeys, reduce transport costs, ease the movement of farm produce, improve trade, and give communities better access to schools, clinics, and markets. Project delivery varies by section and remains ongoing. [4]

Official source
4Expanding

CNG and Lower-Cost Transportation

The Presidential CNG Initiative supports the conversion of commercial vehicles from petrol to compressed natural gas and encourages electric-vehicle adoption. The government reports more than 120,000 vehicles converted, over 400 certified conversion centres, and more than 90 CNG refuelling stations. [3]

What it means for Nigerians: CNG is intended to reduce transport operating costs and emissions over time. The programme is expanding; it does not mean that every citizen has already experienced lower transport fares.

Official source
5Ongoing

Agriculture, Food Security, and Farmer Support

The administration is supporting farmers through improved seedlings, fertiliser, mechanisation, irrigation, finance, and access to markets. It has also highlighted federal agricultural interventions and dry-season farming support across states. [2]

What it means for Nigerians: Higher productivity and stronger market access can improve rural incomes, increase local food production, and strengthen national food security.

Official source
6Completed

Healthcare Renewal and Access

The Federal Government reports that it has achieved all 41 presidential health-sector commitments, with progress in healthcare delivery, financing, infrastructure, local manufacturing, and national health security. [6]

What it means for Nigerians: Stronger primary care, better-equipped facilities, expanded coverage for vulnerable people, and local manufacturing can bring care closer to families and reduce out-of-pocket pressure.

Official source
7Ongoing

Security, Welfare, and Community Safety

The administration continues to invest in security operations, equipment, intelligence, and the welfare, salaries, and conditions of armed forces and police personnel. Its stated objective is stronger protection against terrorism, banditry, kidnapping, and violent crime. [2]

What it means for Nigerians: Safer communities enable families, farmers, traders, and businesses to live, travel, farm, and trade with greater confidence. Security progress remains an ongoing national priority.

Official source
8Ongoing

Increased Resources for States and Local Services

Fiscal reforms are intended to strengthen government revenue and make federal allocations to states more predictable. Stronger revenues can support greater state investment in local services, subject to responsible state and local-government implementation.

What it means for Nigerians: States can invest more in roads, schools, healthcare, agriculture, housing, and other services that people use every day. Actual delivery depends on state and local-government action.

Official source
9Ongoing

Jobs, Enterprise, and Made-in-Nigeria Growth

The administration is supporting employment, small businesses, industrialisation, local production, and consumer finance, including CREDICORP where applicable. The aim is to make essential goods more affordable, strengthen local manufacturers, and expand opportunities for young people and entrepreneurs.

What it means for Nigerians: Stronger local production and enterprise support can create jobs, expand market opportunities, and help households access essential goods more affordably.

Official source
10Expanding

Education, Skills, and Digital Opportunity

The administration is investing in tertiary infrastructure, technical and vocational education, digital skills, research, and youth empowerment. Its education agenda combines student financing with practical skills and improved learning environments.

What it means for Nigerians: Better learning environments, practical skills, innovation, and digital access can create stronger pathways into employment and modern-economy jobs.

Official source
Transparency note: Some reforms involve short-term sacrifices. President Tinubu’s second-term agenda focuses on converting reform into lower living costs, more jobs, stronger public services, safer communities, and shared prosperity.

What This Means for You

Reform is not only about policy. It is about whether a student can stay in school, a farmer can produce more, a trader can plan ahead, and a family can access care and opportunity.

Students

More access to education financing through NELFUND, helping eligible students remain in school and pursue their ambitions. [1]

Farmers

Better inputs, mechanisation, irrigation, finance, and market opportunities can raise productivity and strengthen rural incomes. [2]

Traders and entrepreneurs

A more stable business environment, enterprise support, local production, and access to opportunity can help businesses grow and create jobs.

Commuters and businesses

Improved roads, transport corridors, CNG infrastructure, and connectivity can reduce journey time and support commerce. [3][4]

Families

Stronger healthcare, health financing, and social-support systems can improve access to care and reduce household vulnerability. [6]

Young people

Skills, jobs, digital opportunity, technical education, and enterprise support can open pathways into work and innovation.

Communities

Improved security, public infrastructure, and local services can make communities safer, more connected, and more productive. [2]

Every Nigerian

The road ahead

What President Tinubu Will Do in a Second Term

A second term will focus on turning reform into measurable improvements in daily life, opportunity, and national resilience.

Lower the cost of living

Expand targeted social protection, strengthen food availability, improve transport affordability, and support households through the transition to a more sustainable economy.

Create jobs and support small businesses

Expand access to finance, markets, digital tools, technical skills, and local-production opportunities for young people, artisans, traders, and entrepreneurs.

Complete priority infrastructure

Accelerate delivery of priority roads, transport corridors, power infrastructure, ports, and rural connectivity, with transparent milestones and delivery timelines.

Expand affordable healthcare

Strengthen primary healthcare, hospital upgrades, health insurance coverage, medical supply resilience, and local health-sector manufacturing. [6]

Improve education and digital access

Expand NELFUND, technical and vocational education, digital skills, research, and modern learning infrastructure so more young Nigerians can compete and create. [1]

Strengthen food security

Scale mechanisation, irrigation, improved inputs, extension services, storage, and market access to raise agricultural productivity and reduce food insecurity. [2]

Improve national security

Sustain investment in personnel welfare, equipment, intelligence, community policing, and coordinated operations against terrorism, banditry, kidnapping, and violent crime. [2]

Deepen growth and industrialisation

Attract productive investment, support manufacturing, expand exports, improve infrastructure, and build a more competitive, job-rich Nigerian economy. [4]

Important: These are forward-looking commitments. Their delivery depends on implementation, funding, security conditions, and cooperation with states, local governments, the private sector, and development partners.
